# Design System Console

> The console section that draws your design system at its rendered values — foundations, your own components, brand, voice — and the revocable link that publishes it to someone with no login.

`/_admin/design-system` draws the design system your config declares, at the values it actually renders with. The export endpoints and `sovrium design-system` hand the same content to a machine as text; this is the version a person reads.

It is admin-gated and returns `404` to anyone else, and it is **read-only**. Nothing on it edits configuration — the console is an operational data console, and the design system is one of the things it reflects.

## The pages

The section is the sidebar's **Design** group: Overview, Foundations, Components, Brand and Voice, listed directly. Overview is a page _about_ the design system; the pages after it are pages _of_ it, each drawing your real tokens through the same renderer your own pages use. Two of them exist only when your config declares what they draw: Components answers 404, and leaves no row in the sidebar, on an app that declares no reusable component, and Brand does the same on an app whose `design` declares tokens and no identity.

| Route                                    | What it draws                                                                                                                                                                               | Shown when                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `/_admin/design-system`                  | The system summarised: what `design` declares against the default, one row per facet, and the **Share and export** section at its foot.                                                     | always                                                               |
| `/_admin/design-system/foundations`      | Every token the app renders with — colour, type, spacing, radius, breakpoints, durations — drawn at the value it resolves to in the scheme you are reading, with the type ladder beside it. | always                                                               |
| `/_admin/design-system/components`       | The reusable components your own config declares, grouped by kind, each drawn on its own with where it is used.                                                                             | `components`                                                         |
| `/_admin/design-system/components/:name` | One declared component.                                                                                                                                                                     | `components`                                                         |
| `/_admin/design-system/brand`            | The identity you declared: the logo and its rules, the imagery rules, what each colour is for, and the zone each route family belongs to.                                                   | `design` declares a logo, colour roles, imagery, principles or zones |
| `/_admin/design-system/voice`            | The rules a writer follows here: the principles, how the app addresses a reader, the tone per moment, and the per-zone overrides.                                                           | reads `design.voice`                                                 |

On the Overview, each page row carries a small preview drawn from your own design: your colours (the ones you declare first), the names of your templates, and one sentence of your voice (its first preferred line, else its first personality trait).

The console does not document the engine's own component types. Those are shadcn/ui's, and `sovrium docs components` lists every one of them with its options; the Overview points there once.

Every page renders through the same shell and reads the same endpoints, so what a page is built from is invisible from the outside. There is **one scheme toggle**, in the console chrome, and it switches the specimens and the chrome around them together. `?scheme=dark` deep-links into it. The values printed beside each swatch follow the scheme, so the page never labels a swatch with a colour it is not showing.

Foundations lists colours under shadcn/ui's names (`background`, `card`, `primary`, `muted-foreground`…), in shadcn's order. A key you wrote under an older name shows under its shadcn twin (`background-raised` as `card`), and an extension such as `success` or `primary-hover` shows only when you declare it. Each swatch is filled with its colour and carries its values on it. Each `*-foreground` is measured against the colour it sits on (`primary-foreground` on `primary`), every other colour against `background`. In dark, a colour you did not declare for dark shows the value your app actually paints, marked with where it comes from and measured. A brand colour (`primary`, `secondary`, `accent`, `ring`, a chart colour) you declared for light only keeps your light value, and the text drawn on it keeps its light value too; so does a colour of your own the platform has no dark default for. Every other colour takes the platform's dark default, including a background, card, body text or border you declared for light only, because kept light it would draw dark text on a dark page. When you declare `primary` and neither `primary-hover` nor `primary-active`, both are derived from it: the same hue, a step lighter and a step darker.

## Your components

The Components page is a gallery of the templates in your `components` list, grouped by what each one is — **Page sections**, **Cards**, **Forms**, **Navigation** and **Data views** — read from the type of its outermost component. Each tile draws the template live under your own theme, names it, says on how many pages it is used, and offers **Copy $ref**, which copies the line that places it in a page. A template's own page draws it larger, and at the widths below. A template declared `render: transparent` is furniture rather than a part to review, so the Components pages do not list it.

A template drawn on its own has no `vars`, so the console draws it on **sample data**: each `$variable` takes the value the first page in your `pages` list hands it through `vars`, and a variable no page supplies is drawn as its own name. A template's page also carries a **Props** table: every variable it reads, the type of node it fills, and that sample.

A template is also drawn in the **state its users meet**, on a sample made from your config alone — never a record, an invitation or a person of your instance. A template placed on a page bound to one record reads `$record` from a sample of that table: each text field reads `Sample <field>`, an option field its first option, a date a fixed day. An invitation card is drawn on a pending invitation, a two-step code block on a sign-in waiting for its code, and a record's history on a few changes by a sample person. Every form in a frame can be filled and pressed, and none of them sends anything.

## Seeing a component at three widths

A component page draws its subject at 1280, 768 and 375, stacked and sharing one left edge. Each is a real render at that width, not the same render squeezed: the component's own media queries evaluate against the frame, so a header that collapses to a menu button below `lg` collapses here too. The three frames follow whatever colour scheme you are reading the console in. A frame wider than the column is scaled down to fit it, and its heading says by how much ("shown at 51 %"); the component still lays out at the width the heading names.

In your own config this is `viewport` on a `specimen`:

```yaml
- type: specimen
  subject:
    component: site-header
  viewport:
    width: 375
```

`viewport` needs `subject.component` — a template you declared. It re-renders the subject in a document of its own, which is the only way a width can reach a media query.

## Taking it away with you

The **Share and export** section at the foot of the Overview is the handover: the same system as a document rather than a page. It lists the full bundle first — the one download to take into a design tool or an AI — then its parts one file at a time (the markdown brief, the token document and the theme stylesheet), and the two terminal commands, for the brief and for the bundle. These routes out all read `design` and nothing else.

| Route                                      | What it hands over                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `GET /api/admin/design-system.json`        | A W3C Design Tokens document — **DTCG 2025.10**; `?mode=dark` for the dark palette.            |
| `GET /api/admin/design-system.md`          | A markdown brief, written to be pasted into an AI agent's context.                             |
| `GET /api/admin/design-system.css`         | The theme stylesheet in shadcn/ui variable names: light under `:root`, dark under `.dark`.     |
| `GET /api/admin/design-system.zip`         | The **portable bundle**, as one download (below). `?pages=true` adds your pages.               |
| `GET /api/admin/design-system/bundle`      | The bundle's `manifest.json`: every file with its size, SHA-256 and role. Takes `?pages=true`. |
| `GET /api/admin/design-system/bundle/file` | One text file of the bundle by `?path=`, byte for byte. Fonts are only in the zip.             |
| `sovrium design-system`                    | The same documents offline — no server, no database — and the bundle with `--format bundle`.   |

The CLI takes `--format md` (the default), `--format json` or `--format bundle`, `--mode light` (the default) or `--mode dark` with `--format json` only, and `--output <path>` to write a file instead of stdout, creating parent directories as needed. With `--output` the document goes to the file only; notices go to stderr, so `--format json` leaves stdout parseable. An unrecognised `--format` or `--mode` is an error rather than a silent fall back to the default. The brief lists each colour's light and dark value side by side, one row per colour; the Dark column is what the app paints under `.dark`, so the brief needs no `--mode`.

### The portable bundle

The bundle is one directory for a design tool or an assistant: `DESIGN.md` (the brief), `llms.txt` (an index to read first), `tokens/` (the DTCG documents for light and dark, and a `sovrium.resolver.json` choosing between them), `css/theme.css` and `css/tailwind.css` (a Tailwind v4 `@theme`), `components/` (a rendered kit — one static page per component type, each variant drawn in light and dark, with the component alone and none of the page around it), `fonts/` (the font files the kit uses, copied, never inlined: a face your type scale loads through a `url` in your public directory comes with its stylesheet and every file that stylesheet names, the platform's Geist files are left out when no face uses them, and a face given as an `https://` address is named in the output, never fetched), `assets/` (the sample picture, video and sound the media specimens draw, and your logo files when `design.logo` names files in your public directory — a logo given as an `https://` address is named, never fetched), `adapters/` (the same tokens in each design tool's own format, below), and `manifest.json`, which lists every other file with its SHA-256. The kit loads nothing from the network, so it opens offline. A type it cannot draw as a static page — a live form, one that needs your records — is listed in `components/manifest.json` with the reason, rather than left out. The four overlays that open over a whole page — `sheet`, `dialog`, `alert-dialog` and `drawer` — are drawn open inside a frame of their own, with their heading, their body and an alert's two answers, so the kit shows the panel a row click opens and the question a delete asks; nothing on the page opens or closes them. Each component's entry, and its README in the Claude Design folder, carries the dos and don'ts the component manual states for that type, sentence for sentence; a type the manual gives no such rule is left without any. The light tokens hold the same values as the `.json` export, and the brief is the `.md` export. The token files are written indented, the same on every surface — the CLI's directory, the zip, a shared link and the file read — so one config gives the same files, checksum for checksum, wherever the bundle came from. A bundle is never accepted back: every route above answers `GET` only.

### The pages

With `sovrium design-system --format bundle --pages`, or `?pages=true` on the zip and the manifest read, the bundle also carries your pages, under `pages/`, each file listed with the `page` role:

Five flags make the drawing read as the screens people see, and none changes the default output. `--seed <dir>` draws the pages from your seed files, as the first seeded account of the role each page is drawn for, a secret drawn as `••••••••`, a record page once per seeded record (`--records <n>`), a page routed by a slug field at its seeded records' slugs and a page drawn from a content folder at its first file (either is skipped and named when it has nothing to be drawn at), a table from at most its first 300 rows, each cell written in its column's format (the output names a table it cuts), every value a record prints written as the served page writes it — money, a duration's clock form, the record's created and changed instants — a seeded row reading as created at its `created_at`, else at `--now`, and a formula or rollup drawn from the value its seed record holds, and `--now <instant>` fixes the clock relative times are drawn against. Each page is drawn at its default state (each query at its default, a record rule against the drawn record), and `--state <page>:<key>=<value>` adds a board per state. `--prototype` makes the Claude Design boards clickable, and `--prototype=app` adds one Prototype board whose changes carry across pages, played as the role its `role` Tweak names: it shows only the rows and fields that role may read, and writes only what that role may create or change. An app with several languages is played in the language its `language` Tweak names, and a link carrying a language segment plays its page in that language. Its links, tabs, menus, selects and forms play: a sign-in form signs in as the board's role and opens that role's landing page, keeping nothing typed, a button starting an automation shows its pending label and its success without running anything (the output names it), and a record page's view draws its record, its dates, checkboxes and visibility rules as the page's board does, its edit form changing the record everywhere.

| File                       | What it holds                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `pages/<name>.page.html`   | The page as Sovrium draws it, opening offline with `pages/pages.css`. Every node carries `data-sovrium-id`: the page name, a colon, and the node's position in the page (`dashboard:/components/1/children/0`).        |
| `pages/<name>.tree.json`   | What a design tool may change: each node's type, key, variant and size, its layout (direction, gap, alignment, padding, width, at the base and at each breakpoint, each with the token it is on) and its literal text. |
| `pages/node-map.json`      | What it must never hold: each binding as the expression your config writes, the data source, the visibility rule, the translation keys, the interactions, the page's path and meta, and a content hash per node.       |
| `pages/page-sidecars.json` | For a TypeScript config only: where each page's `<name>.page.json` sidecar goes.                                                                                                                                       |

The attribute is written only in an export: the page a visitor loads never carries it. An image whose file is in your public directory is carried into the bundle and drawn from there, each colour-mode variant with its own file; an image given as an `https://` address keeps it; a `src` naming no file is named in the output by page and node, and the rest is written. A component's own options, such as a form's `headingLevel`, shape the drawing exactly as they shape the page and never appear as attributes on it. The faces your type scale self-hosts are declared in `pages/pages.css` with their files. A template placement is drawn expanded and listed as one locked node, with the template's name and the placement's own `vars` in the node map. A data-bound node is drawn from a small synthetic dataset made from the table's own field definitions — never from a row of your instance: a table bound to one of your tables draws a column per field when it names none, and a list and a kanban bound to one draw its rows as items and cards. A data component the dataset cannot fill, such as a grid, a figure, a chart or a gallery bound to a system endpoint, draws its empty state, never a loading placeholder. Translated and bound text is drawn, but only its key or expression is kept. Without the option the bundle is exactly the bundle above, file for file; with it, every other file stays byte for byte the same. Two exports of one config are identical, file for file: every page is drawn at one fixed sample instant, whatever clock the machine reads or `SOVRIUM_DEV_CLOCK` sets, so a look-back window or a relative date draws the same each time. Every page is drawn as a sample admin would meet it, and every node of its tree is drawn, so none reads back as deleted: a page closed by an `access` rule is drawn like the rest, a page that answers that admin with a redirect (such as the `auth.landingPath` page, which sends a signed-in session on to its role's landing) has nothing to draw, so it is skipped and named in the output with the address it sends to while every other page is written, a node shown only to some readers is drawn whatever its visibility rule says, beside the node shown to the others, and a tab panel that is not open, a dialog's body and an invitation's answer form are drawn in the page, hidden; a tab set is drawn as tabs, one tab list whose every tab names, by `aria-controls`, the `id` of its panel, the hidden ones too; a page whose path takes a parameter (`/records/:table`) is drawn at a sample address — the first table your config declares when the segment names a table, `1` otherwise.

For Figma, each page is also written as `adapters/figma/pages/<name>.figma.json`, listed with the `adapter` role: a page document for the Sovrium Figma plugin, which has no other input. It draws the page as one board at a 375px base width, then one board per breakpoint your layout uses, at that breakpoint's width. A container is a frame with its auto-layout already computed in px (`gap-4` is an item spacing of 16), each spacing and padding bound by name to the variable of the same token in `light.tokens.json` and `dark.tokens.json` (`spacing/4`). A button or any other kit component is an instance carrying its variant and size; a template placement and a data-bound node are locked, the placement carrying its own `vars` and the grid its synthetic rows. Every layer drawn from a node carries that node's position as plugin data, and the document ends with the list of nodes it drew and the hash of each, so a drawing made before a later config change can never remove what it never showed. Like the rest of `pages/`, it holds no binding, translation key, data source, visibility rule, interaction, path or meta: translated text is drawn in your default language, and bound text as the name of the value it reads, in brackets.

For Penpot, every page is also drawn in one file, `adapters/penpot/<app>.penpot`, listed with the `adapter` role, and the manifest's adapter list then names `penpot`. It is a Penpot export as Penpot writes one (a ZIP archive), so it is imported as a file, with no plugin: one Penpot page per page of yours, each holding one board at a 375px width, and inside it a frame or a text per node. A container is a flex frame, its direction, alignment and distribution as your layout sets them and its gap and padding in px (`gap-4` is a gap of 16). The tokens come from `tokens-studio/tokens.json`, as for the rest of the design system. Penpot has no breakpoints, so the board draws your base layout only, and a layout you set at a breakpoint is left as it is by a Penpot round trip. A template placement and a data-bound node are locked frames. Every shape drawn from a node carries that node's position and type as plugin data, and the board carries the list of nodes the export drew with the hash of each. Like the rest of `pages/`, the file holds no binding, translation key, data source, visibility rule, interaction, path or meta. Reading it back with `sovrium design-system import --pages` takes, per node, its order, its own layout on your spacing scale and its literal text; a value off your scale, and a shape that carries no Sovrium tag, are reported, never guessed. A `.penpot` file in Penpot's older binary format is refused: open it in Penpot 2 and export it again.

#### Your pages in Claude Design

With the pages, the bundle also draws them as a Claude Design canvas under `adapters/claude-design/canvas/`, each file listed with the `adapter` role. The folder stands for the canvas's `project/`:

| File                                                                       | What it holds                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `canvas.json`                                                              | The canvas index (version 3): one canvas page per section of your app (its public pages, its sign-in page, the pages every signed-in reader opens, and the pages a few roles open), each a row under a note naming it, its pages 80px apart; a page's other boards (one per seeded record or state) sit under its own. Each board is titled with the page title and is as tall as its page is drawn at its width (a page drawn taller than the canvas's 8000px board limit is named in the output with its height, and its board stops at the limit with a canvas note saying what is cut), and a design-system record names your app. |
| `<page>/<Page>.dc.html`                                                    | One board per page: the page as Sovrium draws it. Every node is marked with its component type (`data-kit`) and its `data-sovrium-id`, plus its variant, size and own layout where it sets them; a locked node is marked as such. A button or a badge is mounted as your design system's own component (`<x-import>`), its variant a prop. Each board's policy lets nothing leave it but the `https` images your config names, and with `--prototype` its links lead to the boards of their targets and its grids, menus, dialogs and forms play.                                                                                      |
| `sovrium-export.json`                                                      | What each board drew when it was exported, so reading the canvas back tells a node you deleted from one your config gained since.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `pages.css`                                                                | The stylesheet the boards are painted with.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `ds/<app>/tokens.json`, `ds/<app>/fonts/`, `ds/<app>/components/bundle.js` | Your design system, installed in the canvas as the format installs one, so the colour pickers and text styles offer your tokens and the boards mount its components.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

Publish the Claude Design project first, then open the canvas and let it install that published project in place of the record the export wrote: the export cannot know the address the project gets. A board loads nothing from the network but an image your config itself gives as an `https://` address. It never carries a translation key, an interaction or a network address: a link points at `#` (or its own `#fragment`), and text that reads your data shows a placeholder such as `[name]`. What a component does travels beside it instead, in your config's own words: `data-sovrium-bind` holds the text a binding fills, `data-sovrium-visible` its visibility rule and `data-sovrium-source` its data source as JSON, `data-sovrium-form` the form it embeds, and `data-sovrium-href` on a button the page it opens. A link to a page of your app names where it leads, so the boards can be wired into a prototype: `data-sovrium-href` holds the address as your config writes it (`/records/tickets`, or a `?query` on the same page), and `data-sovrium-page` the name of the page that address draws, route parameters matched. A link to another site keeps no trace of its address. Reading the canvas back never reads a link's two attributes; the drawings under `pages/` keep the real addresses. Each board also carries the list of nodes the export drew, with a content hash of each, so that reading the canvas back can tell a node a designer deleted from one your config gained since. Reading it back with `sovrium design-system import --pages` takes, per node, its order, its type, its variant and size, its own layout on your spacing scale (also when set in its `class` list, or in the properties panel as `gap`, `padding`, `flex-direction`, `align-items`, `justify-content` or `width`), a colour or a text size that is one of your tokens, its literal text, and the rules above. A value off your scale or your tokens, a class or a style the import does not read, and anything drawn that is not a component of the kit are reported, never guessed.

A board drawn for a page your config does not declare comes back as a new page with `--new-pages`. The page is named after the page its root names (`data-sovrium-page`), whatever the board file is called, and sits at the path its root's `data-sovrium-path` gives (else `/<name>`). A path that is no page path, or that a page or another new board already takes, is refused naming the board, and nothing is written, in a dry run as with `--write`. The rules drawn on its nodes come with them, as on an edited board; a node whose rule names a table, field, form or page your config does not declare is refused by name and left out, and the rest of the page is created. In a TypeScript config the page is appended to the `pages` array your config's own module writes, nothing else changing; when `pages` is computed there instead, the import is refused naming the file and line, and `--sidecar` writes the page to a file of its own. Writing the same canvas back a second time changes nothing: the board is then read as a drawing of the page it created.

Taking the system away is recorded in the audit log: each download of the zip, each read of the manifest and each read of the stylesheet writes one design read event naming you, as the `.json` and `.md` exports do. Reading the bundle one file at a time writes none — the manifest read that lists those files already records the handover.

### `?flat=1` — the same tokens, addressed by path

The DTCG document is a nested tree, which is right for a conformant consumer and awkward for a script that wants one value. Adding `flat` to the **JSON** endpoint returns `{ items, total }` instead: one row per token, each addressed by its dotted path.

```bash
curl -H 'Cookie: <admin session>' \
  'http://localhost:3000/api/admin/design-system.json?flat=1'
```

Two things to know. It is opt-in **by name, not by value** — `?flat`, `?flat=0` and `?flat=1` all switch the shape, because a parameter that reshaped a standards document depending on its value would be a trap for the consumer that appended a cache-buster. And it is a **projection, never a replacement**: without the flag the document is byte-identical to what it always was. The markdown endpoint ignores the flag entirely.

## The share link

A design system's audience is wider than the people who have an account on your instance: designers, an agency, a client stakeholder. `GET /s/design-system/{token}` gives them the charter without giving them a login. The **Share link** panel mints one. The link is unlisted — it appears in no sitemap, no navigation and no page listing — and it is revocable.

| Operation | Endpoint                                      | Notes                                                                      |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mint      | `POST /api/admin/design-system/shares`        | `201` with the id, the creation time, the token and the URL.               |
| List      | `GET /api/admin/design-system/shares`         | `{ items, total }`. Live shares, **metadata only** — id and creation time. |
| Revoke    | `DELETE /api/admin/design-system/shares/{id}` | `200` once; `404` for an unknown or already-revoked id.                    |
| Read      | `GET /s/design-system/{token}`                | Anonymous. No login, no cookie, no account.                                |
| Download  | `GET /s/design-system/{token}.zip`            | Anonymous. The same bundle an admin downloads; dies with the link.         |

**The token is shown exactly once**, in the response to the mint. It is 256 bits of randomness — 64 hex characters — and it is stored only as a SHA-256 digest, so nothing, not the list endpoint, not the audit log, not a log line, can hand it back. That is what makes revocation mean something: an operator who could re-read a link would never learn it had leaked. If you lose the link, revoke it and mint another.

Revoking is immediate and total. The reader gets the same `404` as an unknown or malformed token — never a "this link has been revoked", which is a different answer and exactly what an enumerator collects. The reader page is a single self-contained document: no script, no form, no comment box, no upload. There is nothing on it to interact with, which is the strongest form of "the widest audience gets the weakest surface".

### What the link publishes

Anyone holding the URL reads the whole design system, not just its tokens. Before you send one, know that it contains:

- your **principles** and your **logo** section, including its clear-space, minimum-size and misuse rules;
- your **voice** — personality, pronoun, what to prefer, what to avoid — and the **tone** instruction for each moment;
- every **colour token** with its value, its usage rule and the token it pairs with;
- the **type scale**, and the spacing, radius, breakpoint, font and duration tokens;
- your **imagery** rules and the icon set;
- the **usage, when and don't** guidance for each of your components;
- the values Sovrium kept outside the token tree, and anything you declared that it does not apply.
- as a **download**, the whole portable bundle: the token files for both colour modes, the stylesheets and the rendered component kit — files a reader keeps after you revoke the link. It never carries your pages, even when the reader asks for them with `?pages=true`: only an administrator's own download does.

It contains no environment-variable value and no record data — the projection reads `design` and nothing else, so there is no path on which a secret could arrive. But a brand charter is a document about your product, and a public link to it is a decision. Treat minting one as publishing.

Both mint and revoke write to the audit log — a mint at `warning` severity, because it is the entry an operator scans for when reconstructing when something became public. Neither entry records the token.
